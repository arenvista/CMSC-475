## 1. The Translation Task: Encoder-Decoder Architecture

Consider a model designed to translate a sequence from Spanish to English.

* **Model:** $f(x) := \text{Transformer}$
* **Input ($x$):** "Me encanta UMBC"
* **Target ($y$):** "I love UMBC"

Transformers rely on an **Encoder-Decoder** structure. The Encoder processes the input to capture its meaning, and the Decoder uses that meaning to generate the output. 

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 
    \\ 
    \text{Encoder Matrix } E \\ 
    \\ 
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \\ \vec{x} \\ \\ \end{bmatrix} = 
\begin{bmatrix} \\ \vec{z} \\ \\ \end{bmatrix}
\quad \quad \quad
\begin{bmatrix}
    \\ 
    \text{Decoder Matrix } D \\ 
    \\ 
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \\ \vec{z} \\ \\ \end{bmatrix} = 
\begin{bmatrix} \\ \hat{\vec{y}} \\ \\ \end{bmatrix}
$$

$$E \vec{x} = \vec{z} \quad \quad \quad D \vec{z} = \hat{\vec{y}} \approx \vec{y}$$

*(Note: While an autoencoder aims for reconstruction where $\hat{\vec{x}} \approx \vec{x}$, a translation model aims for generation where the prediction $\hat{\vec{y}}$ approximates the target $\vec{y}$.)*

---

## 2. Autoregressive Generation

While the Encoder can process the entire input sequence $\vec{x}$ simultaneously, the Decoder generates the output $\vec{y}$ **sequentially** (autoregressively). It uses the previously generated tokens as context for the next token.

| Input Context (Fed to Decoder) | Output Prediction |
| :--- | :--- |
| `[START]` | "I" |
| `[START] I` | "love" |
| `[START] I love` | "UMBC" |
| `[START] I love UMBC` | `[STOP]` |

## 3. Positional Encoding

Because Transformers process all tokens in a sequence simultaneously (lacking the inherent sequential nature of Recurrent Neural Networks), we must explicitly inject information about the order of the words. We do this by adding a Positional Encoding (PE) vector to each word embedding.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    PE(pos, 2i)   &= \sin\left( \frac{pos}{10000^{\frac{2i}{d}}} \right) \\
    PE(pos, 2i+1) &= \cos\left( \frac{pos}{10000^{\frac{2i}{d}}} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Variables:**
* $pos$: The position of the token in the input sequence.
* $d$: The total dimensionality of the embedding vector.
* $i$: The specific dimension index within that vector (e.g., if $d = 512 \implies i \in [0, 255]$).
* $10000$: An arbitrarily large base scaling factor.

**Wavelengths (A geometric progression):**
* **Smaller $i$ (Start of vector):** Smaller wavelength $\implies$ **more** oscillations. This helps the model differentiate between nearby words.
* **Larger $i$ (End of vector):** Longer wavelength $\implies$ **fewer** oscillations. This provides a broader sense of the word's general position in the sentence.

## 4. The Need for Context (Encoder Details)

If we were to use a naive approach, we would pass each word independently through a Fully Connected Layer (FCL):

$$
\begin{gathered}
[\text{"Me"}] \xrightarrow{\text{FCL}} [\vec{z}_1] \\ 
[\text{"encanta"}] \xrightarrow{\text{FCL}} [\vec{z}_2] \\ 
[\text{"UMBC"}] \xrightarrow{\text{FCL}} [\vec{z}_3] 
\end{gathered}
$$

**The Problem:** This lacks context. Words are processed in isolation. The word "encanta" is represented identically regardless of what surrounds it.
**The Solution:** We construct a **Self-Attention Layer** to blend context across tokens, allowing words to dynamically influence each other's representations.

---

## 5. The Self-Attention Mechanism

The goal of Self-Attention is to map an input sequence $X = [\vec{x}_1, \vec{x}_2, \vec{x}_3]$ to a context-aware output sequence $Z = [\vec{z}_1, \vec{z}_2, \vec{z}_3]$. 

To do this, we must determine an attention weight matrix $P$, where each entry $p_{i,j}$ indicates the "importance" or relationship between word $x_i$ and word $x_j$.

### The Intuition: The "Search Engine" Analogy
To calculate these relationships, we project our input embeddings into three distinct mathematical spaces using learnable weight matrices ($W^Q, W^K, W^V$). 

1.  **Query ($Q$): "What am I looking for?"** When the model is processing a specific word, that word acts as a Query. It asks the rest of the sentence, "Does anyone have information relevant to me?"
2.  **Key ($K$): "What do I have to offer?"**
    Simultaneously, every word acts as a Key. It holds up a tag describing the information it contains. 
3.  **Value ($V$): "What is my actual content?"**
    If a Query matches a Key, the model retrieves the corresponding Value.

*Similarity (The Dot Product):* If a word's Query strongly matches another word's Key, they have high similarity. The model decides that these two words are highly relevant and "pays attention" to the Value of that match.

### The Mathematical Pipeline

**Step 1: Calculate Q, K, and V**
We multiply the input matrix $X$ by our distinct, learnable weight matrices to create the Queries, Keys, and Values:

$$Q = XW^Q \quad \quad K = XW^K \quad \quad V = XW^V$$

*Even though $Q$ and $K$ both originate from $X$, they represent completely different things. $Q$ is what a word is searching for, and $K$ is the descriptor of what the word contains.*

**Step 2: Calculate Similarity Scores**
To find the similarities between all Queries and all Keys simultaneously, we use matrix multiplication. We multiply the Query matrix $Q$ by the **transpose** of the Key matrix ($K^T$):

$$\text{Scores} = QK^T$$

**Step 3: Convert to Probabilities (The $P$ Matrix)**
Raw dot products can result in unbounded numbers. To stabilize the gradients during training, we scale the scores down by the square root of the key dimension ($\sqrt{d_k}$). Then, we apply a **Softmax** function across the rows so that the attention weights for each word sum to 1. This gives us our probability matrix $P$:

$$P = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)$$

**Step 4: Compute the Final Output ($Z$)**
Finally, we multiply our attention matrix $P$ by our Values matrix $V$. For any given word, its final context-aware vector is the sum of all Values in the sentence, weighted by their attention probabilities:

$$Z = PV$$

### Bringing It All Together
The entire Self-Attention mathematical operation can be written cleanly as:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V = Z
$$
