# Probability Modeling of Word Sequences

Given a probability of $n$ words, can we model the probability of that sequence?

### Joint Probability

$$
\text{Corpus} \rightarrow \underset{\tiny{\text{Vocabulary}}}{\begin{bmatrix} w_1 \\ w_2 \\ w_3 \\ w_4 \\ \end{bmatrix}} \implies \exists~ 4\ \text{combinations}
$$

Find the occurrences of words in the vocabulary.

### Conditional Probability (Next Word Prediction)
Find the word $w_5$ that goes after $w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4$, given $w_1, \dots ,w_4$ are in the context window.

$$
P(w_5|
\underset{w_1}{\text{Mary}}~
\underset{w_2}{\text{had}}~
\underset{w_3}{\text{a}}~
\underset{w_4}{\text{little}})
$$

$$
\begin{align}
P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_5)
&= P(w_1)P(w_2|w_1) \\
&\times P(w_3|w_1, w_2) \\
&\times P(w_4|w_1, w_2, w_3) \\
&\times P(w_5|w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4)
\end{align}
$$

## Markov Assumption

$$
\begin{gathered}
P(\text{blue}~|~\text{The water of Walden Pond is so beautifully}) \\
\approx \\
P(\text{blue}~|~\text{beautifully})
\end{gathered}
$$

Generally:
$$
P(w_n|w_{1:n-1}) \approx P(w_n|w_{n-1})
$$

### Bigram Markov Assumption
$$
P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n) \approx \prod_{k=1}^n P(w_k|w_{k-1})
$$

#### Bigram Example
$$
\begin{gathered}
P(\underset{w_1}{\text{Mary}}~
\underset{w_2}{\text{had}}~
\underset{w_3}{\text{a}}~
\underset{w_4}{\text{little}})
= P(\text{Mary}) \\
\times P(\text{had}|\text{Mary}) \\
\times P(\text{a}|\text{had}) \\
\times P(\text{little}|\text{a})
\end{gathered}
$$

#### Unigram Model
$$
P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n) \approx \prod_{i=1}^n P(w_i)
$$

## Perplexity
The best model predicts the best on an unseen test set: a model that gives the highest $P(\text{sentence})$.
