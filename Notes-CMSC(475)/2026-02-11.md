Recall: 
$$
\begin{align}
    a_1 & = w_1x + b_1 \\
    o_1 & = f(a_1) =  f(w_1x + b_1)
\end{align}
$$ 
Where $a_1$ is the output of the linear function. 

Where $o_1$ is the actual output of the layer. 
- Passes $a_1$ via a non-linear function to resolve the "XOR" problem. 


# Loss
We need to compute partial dir' to find the change in loss

$$
    \frac{dL}{d \theta}  = \frac{dL}{dw_1} 
$$ 

Finding the loss associated to the weight:
$$
    \frac{dL}{dw_3} = \frac{dL}{df_3}\frac{df_3}{da_3}\frac{a_3}{dw_3}
$$ 


$$
    -n(y-\hat{y})\frac{df_3}{da_3}\frac{a_3}{w_3}
$$ 

Where $df_3$ to some loss function for example the sigmoid function: 


> This process is backpropigation and is automatically handled in libraries such as pytorch 

# Gradient Descent 

> [!def] Gradient Descent 
> For each sample $\{ x_i, y_i \}$
> 1. Predict 
>     - Forward Pass $\hat{y} = f_{MLP}(x_i; \theta)$
>     - Compute Loss $L_i$
> 2. Update 
>     - Backpropigation $\frac{dL}{d\theta}$
>     - Gradient Update: $w_3 = w_3 - n \nabla w_3 = \theta \leftarrow -n \frac{dL}{d \theta}$

> [!tip] Vector Field
> $$
> \nabla J (\theta_1, \theta_2) = \begin{bmatrix}
>     \frac{dJ}{d\theta_1} \\ \frac{dJ}{d \theta_2}
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$ 
> This vector is evaluated at the point $[\theta_1, \theta_2]^T$ centered at the origin. 

> [!def] Update Rule
> Broadly we can define the update rule as: 
> $$
>     w = w - \nabla w
> $$ 
> "Go down the steepest path"


There are many possible ways to detect **convergence**. 

> [!cor] Stopping 
> We could check the $L_2$ norm of the gradient below. Once the change in loss is sufficently small then we can decide to define a threshold to stop training. 
> $$
>     || \nabla_{\theta} J(\theta)||_2 \leq  \epsilon
> $$
